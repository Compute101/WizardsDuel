The Known World, a first sketch

Everything built so far (Wrenford to Corvanthe and the Moriquende) is the small green-ringed patch on Albra, top centre. Roads have a town or an inn at least every day’s ride by cart; tap a town to see why it stands there and how many days it is to the next. The isle is half its first size and the Mainland about twice, so the Mainland is roughly Europe beside Britain. Tap any region or place to read what lives there and what could happen there. Names with a “?” are placeholders. The Mainland’s countries are tinted, with dashed borders; the purple dotted lines are gnome airship routes.

How this version is drawn

  1. Rivers run from high ground to the sea. They rise in the Anvilfells, the Greatspine, the Sunwall or the western uplands, join into trees, and end in estuaries or deltas. The Hollow rises in the western uplands and runs east past Wrenford’s Wren and Hollowmere into Corvanthe and out to the Wolf Sea, like the Thames past Oxford and London.
  2. The Greatspine is the watershed. Its north side drains to the Narrows, the Wolf Sea and the Salt Sea, its south side to the Middle Sea. Two passes cross it, and the main roads use them.
  3. Cities sit where cities grow: river mouths, harbours, where rivers meet, and the foot of a pass. Roads follow valleys and coasts.
  4. The coast has shape: three peninsulas into the Middle Sea, the inland Salt Sea, lakes at the mountains’ feet, Westholm off Albra, and fjord islands in the cold north.
  5. Climate: the west is wet and wooded, the north-east steppe dry, the Middle Sea coast dry scrub and olives, and the Southlands’ desert lies in the Sunwall’s rain shadow.
