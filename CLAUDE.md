# WizardsDuel — Notes for Claude

## What this is
A browser-based 1v1 wizard duel game. Each round both wizards secretly pick a Damage, Protection or Dispel spell (rock-paper-scissors), cast it via a short glyph minigame, then the picks are revealed and play out together. Built with vanilla JS/Canvas, no framework. Main logic lives in `js/game.js` (~5700 lines). Character stats and each character's three signature spells are in `characters.json`.

## The Arcana Alphabet

Every spell's casting minigame uses glyphs from a shared 12-symbol alphabet. This was designed deliberately — do not change the symbols without considering the full system.

### The 12 glyphs

| # | Glyph | Unicode | Name | Shape |
|---|-------|---------|------|-------|
| 1 | ϟ | U+03DF | Koppa | zigzag bolt |
| 2 | Δ | U+0394 | Delta | triangle up |
| 3 | ∇ | U+2207 | Nabla | triangle down |
| 4 | Ψ | U+03A8 | Psi | trident / fork |
| 5 | Ω | U+03A9 | Omega | horseshoe arch |
| 6 | ∞ | U+221E | Lemniscate | figure-eight |
| 7 | ☽ | U+263D | Crescent | open curve |
| 8 | ✸ | U+2738 | Octagram | 8-pointed star |
| 9 | ⊕ | U+2295 | Circled plus | cross in circle |
| 10 | ⊗ | U+2297 | Circled cross | X in circle |
| 11 | θ | U+03B8 | Theta | H-bar in circle |
| 12 | Φ | U+03A6 | Phi | V-bar through circle |

**All 12 are text-only — no emoji presentation variants.** Never replace any with a symbol from the U+2600–U+26FF Miscellaneous Symbols block without checking for emoji variants first (e.g. ⚡ U+26A1 looks like a bolt but renders as a colour emoji on iOS — that's why ϟ is used instead).

### Spell glyph assignments (4 per spell)

| Spell | Element | Glyphs | Notes |
|-------|---------|--------|-------|
| Inferno | fire | `ϟ Δ ⊕ Ω` | bolt / rising flame / solar / consuming end |
| Lightning | lightning | `Ψ ∇ ⊗ ✸` | forked strike / descent / impact / burst |
| Ice | ice | `θ Φ ☽ ∞` | crystal / polar axis / frost moon / eternal |
| Arcane | arcane | `Ω ∞ Ψ θ` | cosmos / eternity / spirit / hidden |
| Dispel | dispel | `∞ ⊕ ∇ θ` | eternal cycle / unbinding / descent / dissolution |
| Physical (Elemental rules) | physical | `Δ ∇ Ψ ⊕` | stone layers / fork / binding — Rockfall minigame (rune stones fall into shuffled spots on a quaking floor) |
| Dark (Elemental rules) | dark | `☽ ⊗ ∇ Ω` | the Necromancy set — Shadow Circle minigame (runes rise from a purple moon circle and orbit it) |

Arcane deliberately shares one glyph with each other element — it's the underlying force of all magic, and the hardest set to memorise in hard mode. Dispel similarly draws one glyph from each elemental set (∞ from Ice, ⊕ from Inferno, ∇ from Lightning, θ from Ice/Arcane), fitting its role as a universal spell that dissolves any type of magic.

## Minigame difficulty system

Each spell's minigame has three difficulty modes. Difficulty is set globally in `diffName` (`'easy'` / `'normal'` / `'hard'`).

**Pacing (all minigames):** 5 s to watch (`MG_WATCH_MS`), then 5 s to enter (`MG_INPUT_S`). Sequence length comes from cast strength, not difficulty: `spell.tier` 1 / 2 / 3 (inner / middle / outer strength ring) means 2 / 3 / 4 glyphs. The tile-match uses 2 / 3 / 4 pairs. With no tier, a cast uses the middle ring. The shared helpers `mgSeqLen` / `mgSeq` / `mgSpawnInterval` sit just above `launchBuffTileMatch`.

**Ending (all minigames):** every minigame ends through `mgEnd(ok, info, cb)` instead of calling `puzzleFinish` directly, and never cuts straight back to the battle. Failure holds for `MG_FAIL_MS` (1.3 s): the canvas greys out and shakes, the wrong key is crossed out in red, the key that was wanted pulses gold, the slot row shows the rest of the answer, and a FIZZLED banner gives the reason (`wrong` / `forbidden` / `timeout` / `strikes`, see `MG_REASONS`). Phones also vibrate. Success holds for `MG_WIN_MS` (0.75 s) with a gold flash and SPELL WOVEN. During input, glyph games draw the sequence as a row of slots (`mgDrawSlots`) that fill in as you tap. Pass the reason (and the wrong glyph) to `finish(ok, reason, wrongAi)`.

### Inferno (launchPatternEcho) — the reference implementation
The Inferno minigame was redesigned as an animated rising-glyph mechanic and serves as the model for how the new system should work in all spells.

**Watch phase:**
- Red glyphs (noise) rise continuously from the bottom — visual distraction.
- White glyphs (the sequence) rise one at a time, spread evenly across the 5 s watch window, with a coloured glow unique to each glyph and a small order-number badge.
- Every sequence glyph stays on screen for at least half the watch window (`MG_GLYPH_MS` = 2.5 s): the last one spawns at the halfway mark. Glyph motion and fades are time-based (ms), not per-frame, so high-refresh screens don't speed them up.

**Input phase — difficulty rules:**

| Mode | Keyboard | Fixed or random |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| Easy | 3×4 grid (all 12 glyphs), non-spell glyphs greyed out | Fixed canonical word (first 2–4 glyphs, by strength) |

| Hard | 3×4 grid, nothing greyed out — all 12 active; a wrong glyph fails | Random from spell's 4 |

### Dispel (launchDispelPattern) — Veil Unravelling
Made harder than the others on purpose (Dispels were too easy). The sequence glyphs are unveiled **one at a time** (fade in / hold / fade out, `MG_GLYPH_MS` each) in **shuffled slots** of a 6-slot inner ring that rotates, with **no order badges** — the order is only shown by when each one appears. Brighter, faster noise rings stay up for most of the watch, and violet **decoy flares** of Dispel's own glyphs appear in the middle ring (2 / 3 / 5 on easy / normal / hard), so the greyed-out keyboard can't rule them out.

**Easy mode canonical words** (the fixed sequence a player can learn for each spell):

| Spell | Canonical sequence |
|-------|--------------------|
| Inferno | ϟ Δ ⊕ Ω (in that order) |
| Lightning | Ψ ∇ ⊗ ✸ (in that order) |
| Ice | θ Φ ☽ ∞ (in that order) |
| Arcane | Ω ∞ Ψ θ (in that order) |
| Dispel | ∞ ⊕ ∇ θ (in that order) |

## Round flow & RPS redesign
Every round goes: secret picks → simultaneous reveal → resolution → round-end ticks. Wards follow the tabletop rule: no round timer — a ward lasts until it runs out. Like the tabletop ward's 1 wink a round, it loses a flat `ELEM_WARD_WEAR` (4 HP) at every round end, and strikes, ticks and dispels wear it too; Eldrin's Ward (`holdWard` on its effect) stops the round-end wear (`ELEM_WARD_ROUNDS` 0, `ELEM_WARD_DECAY` 1; last balance run 47.0–54.5% across the roster). Most buffs have no round timer either, as in tabletop (`untimedBuff`: `addEffect` sets `rounds:null`): a buff lasts until a Dispel strips it, a "next strike" buff until it's used. A buff whose effect has `timed:true` keeps its `rounds` and shows them on its status tag: the ones that pay out every round (Regen, Simulacrum, Danse Macabre), whose value grows without limit with no timer, the short bursts (Frenzy, Blink, Vanish), and Eldrin's Ward, which carries `holdWard` (without a timer it would freeze his Shield's wear for good). Those keep their original numbers. The untimed ones are the armour (War Paint, Stonesoul, Iron Patience, Frost Armor, Dragon Scales), Counter, Arcane Weave, blades and the "next strike" buffs (Empower, Galvanize, Foresight, Infernal Mantle). Curses keep their timers. `BUFF_TIMERS` true gives every buff its `rounds` back. The Arcane unweave rider takes a round off a timed buff and frays an untimed one (newest, or all from the outer ring; `ELEM_UNWEAVE_FRAYS` 2 frays unravel it, 🌀 on the status tag), and the AI leans harder on dispelling untimed buffs (`buffPull` in `elemAIChoose`). The untimed armour was cut to match (War Paint 33→15%, Stonesoul 35→25%, Iron Patience 30→22%, Frost Armor 30→17% / 4→3, Dragon Scales 25→12% / 4→3; Vanish cost 3→4, since back to 3), and Regen and Simulacrum cost 3, Divine Heal 5, Arcane Weave 3. Sim data behind the split (Normal AI): with timers, armour lasted about 2.5 rounds and expired 60–70% of the time; untimed it lasts 4–5.5 rounds and 75% end to a Dispel, so a Dispel takes over the timer's job. Mordant was then lowest (about 43%), so his Agony lasts 5 rounds (was 4); one extra round on all three of his hexes overshot to 64%, and any one alone brought him to about 50%. Ponder's Vanish is timed and isn't used up: while it lasts every strike at him misses (`absorbNext` with `keep:true`, checked first in `strikeResolve`; 2 rounds, 1 / 2 / 3 by ring; cost 3). Foresight is still used up by the next strike; it blocks 1 / 2 / 3 strikes by ring (`charges` = tier, set in `sigAtTier`, one used per blocked strike, ×n on the status tag) It also blocks a damage-over-time tick (`dot` effects) or a foe's minion strike at round end, each using a charge (`foreseeBlock` in `elemTickEffects`); Agony-type curses aren't blocked. Cost 4 at the middle ring (2 / 4 / 6 by ring), with Aurelia's HP cut 90→82 instead of a higher cost: Aurelia 49.4% (HP 85 gave 54.5%, 80 gave 48.4%; at HP 90, cost 4 gave 56.6% and cost 5 53.8%). No striking from hiding: if he picks a Damage spell, `revealVanished` drops the Vanish as the round's picks resolve (in `resolveSimRound`, before any stage), so he's exposed that whole round. The AI barely strikes a Vanished foe and leans toward dispelling it, and a Vanished Ponder rarely strikes. Vanish costs 2. Ponder 49.9% after the change (roster 42.7–57.0%, Fizzwick and Durin at the edges). Fizzwick's Volatile Tonic is no longer a coin flip: below `TONIC_HURT` (50%) of max HP it heals 20, otherwise it empowers his next strike +50%; cost 3→4 (at 3 he hit 59%). At a 70% cutoff he was about 54%; compared one at a time, 50% (49.0%) and Caustic Bomb's hit 8→6 (49.1%) were the cleanest nerfs, heal 16 overshot (44.8%) and the empower size barely mattered. With the 50% cutoff: Fizzwick 49.2%, roster 44.9–56.2%. Later, the ward-bracing moved from Counter to Eldrin's Ward (3 rounds, 4 from the outer ring via `sigAtTier`; immune to curses, his ward doesn't wear, the next dispel only removes the Ward; cost 2), and Counter became an untimed buff (strikers take 5, cost 3; was 10 for 3 rounds with the brace). Last balance run 44.0–56.2% (Frederick and Weizan top; Valdris and Malachar bottom), Eldrin 50.3%, Mordant 51.4%. Resolution runs in stages (the "ELEMENTAL DUEL" section): every Protection, then every Dispel, then every Damage. Each character spell's `el` block in `characters.json` defines it (category, element sector it takes over on that wheel, kind, middle-ring cost and effect — `sigAtTier` scales the other rings). Keep one signature spell per category + element per character. A character's `forbidden` elements (greyed out on its wheel) are a balancing lever: restrict strong characters, never their signature elements. After changing any numbers, re-run `tools/balance.html` and keep every character within ±8% of 50% with the Normal AI. (The old "Classic" rules — per-character spell bars, Channel, free basic attacks — were removed. Meditate — skip your spell for +3 mana, not two rounds in a row — is the Elemental replacement for Channel.) That flow is the "ROUND FLOW" section of `js/game.js`. Loop over `PLAYERS` and `gs.pending`; don't hard-code p1/p2 in new round logic. The agreed Damage / Protection / Dispel design, and the phases still to build, are in `docs/rps-duel-design.md`.

**Dispel nerf:** plain Dispel power is 15 / 24 / 40 by ring (was 15 / 30 / 50; the inner ring stays at 15 so it still breaks a seal, `sealSoak`), and a dispel in the ward's own element does 1.5× (`ELEM_DISPEL_MATCH`, was 2×; the hit is rounded). Curses' ward hits follow, since they're half a dispel's power. Weaker dispels put Durin, who wards a lot, at 59%, so his HP went 110 → 105. 12 / 22 / 36 was too far (Durin 59% again, and a Lesser dispel could no longer break a seal). Balance run (200 games): 44.0–58.1% (Sylvara to Weizan), Durin 53.7%; it was 44.1–57.6% before.

**Wheel screen** (`openElemWheel`): only the title, mana, the wheel, the self buttons (Seal Ward / Cleanse Self) and Cancel. The ring costs (`#wheel-legend`), the category rules (`#wheel-rule`) and the signature spell descriptions (`#wheel-sig`) sit in a help panel (`#wheel-help`) opened by the ⓘ button by the title; keep new explanatory text there, not on the wheel. Hovering a segment shows its name and cost (SVG `<title>`).

**Cleanse Self** has the wheel's three rings, like the tabletop Normal-rules Cleanse: three buttons under the Dispel wheel (`#wheel-cleanse`) clear 1 / 2 / every curse (`CLEANSE_COUNT`) for that ring's Dispel cost (1 / 2 / 4), worst first (`cursesWorstFirst`, ranked by `CURSE_HARM`: Agony, then DoTs and Silence, …; ties go to the newest). Names `CLEANSE_NAMES` (Lesser Cleanse / Cleanse / Greater Cleanse). The smart AI picks the ring that matches its curse count (`cleanse()` in `elemAIChoose`). It used to clear only the newest curse. Balance run (200 games): 43.2–56.7%, was 43.5–57.2%.

**Layered wards** (`docs/rps-duel-design.md` "Layered wards"): switches in `WARD_RULES`. Recasting a layer's element tops it up (`wardBoostTo`: +the cast's HP, up to `boostCap` 1.5 × it); it keeps its `wardSig`, and Blink's side-ward never boosts or layers (`raiseWard`'s `noBoost`; pass a signature ward's id as `raiseWard`'s `sig`, don't set `wardSig` after). A new element goes on over the ward, up to `layers` (2). The outer layer is the old single-ward fields, so "the ward" everywhere means the one you'd hit; the layers beneath are `st.wardInner` (innermost first). Never clear a ward by zeroing its fields: use `breakWard` / `clearOuterWard` (the next layer steps out) or `clearAllWards`, and `wardTotal` / `wardLabel` for the whole stack. Strikes spill through layers; a lesser dispel stops at the layer it breaks, a Greater one carries through and (`dispelStrips`) strips buffs if it breaks a 2+ stack; every layer wears at round end. The AI tops up below `WARD_BOOST_AI_HP` (25) and layers with weight `WARD_LAYER_AI`. Seals (`seals`): 🔒 Seal Ward is the Protection wheel's self button (like Cleanse Self on Dispel), a Protection pick with `self:true` (`isSeal`, `SEAL.cost` 1, `executeSeal`); it seals the outer layer (`st.wardSealed`, `sealed` on a layer beneath). Sealed layers don't wear; a dispel only breaks the seal unless it's a Greater one, which goes on through; strikes ignore seals; a seal blocks the whole dispel, Greater included (`sealBlocks`): it breaks the seal and stops, so a sealed ward takes two dispels; a dispel weaker than `sealSoak` (15, a curse's ward hit) doesn't even break it. The AI breaks seals with its cheapest strong-enough ring. Eldrin's `sealAll` seals every layer; `sealCostMod` changes a character's cost. Gold Arcana glyph ring: `drawWardSeal`. Seal presets and per-run `charMods` are in `WARD_PRESETS`. Compare rule sets with `node tools/ward_compare.js 40 off,boost` (or the **Wards** selector in `tools/balance.html`); add new presets to `WARD_PRESETS` there. The AI seals unsealed wards of `WARD_SEAL_AI_HP` (15)+ HP with weight `WARD_SEAL_AI` (1.0), about 0.4–1.2 times a game. Frederick's HP went 93 → 90 when layers pushed him to 57–59%. Blocking seals put Eldrin top (56.5–57.4% at HP 90); he's an elf, so frail: HP 90 → 82 (84: 50.8%, 82: 48.4%, 80: 45.9%), level with Aurelia at the low end of the roster. Last run (boost, 2 layers, blocking seals, Eldrin `sealAll`, HP 82): 43.5–56.9%, about 22 rounds.

**3–4 wizard games:** AI seats get a random wizard by default (`multiSetup.aiPick`, the **AI Wizards: Random / I Choose** row on the setup screen; `fillRandomAISeats` fills them after each player's pick, and Back skips over them). In free-for-all (`isFreeForAll`) the AI doesn't chase the weakest wizard (that had every AI ganging up on one seat, usually the player): `ffaPickTarget` picks a weighted-random foe, leaning toward the weak, a foe it can finish off and a grudge (whoever aimed at it last round), and away from a foe the others already went for. 2 v 2 keeps focus fire. In the sim this cut rounds where every AI aimed at the same wizard from 44% to 16%. Each wizard's targeting personality is its `aiStyle` in `characters.json` (`AI_STYLES`: vulture Malachar / Frederick, grudge Durin (long memory, `gs[who].grudges`), chivalrous Eldrin / Mary, chaotic Cinder / Fizzwick, calculating Thessaly / Zakkit; everyone else `default`). `rivals` (foe key → taunt) pull it toward those foes (`RIVAL_PULL` ×2, free-for-all only), and the reveal card shows the taunt when a spell is aimed at a rival, in any mode (`rivalTaunt`, at most once every `RIVAL_TAUNT_GAP` rounds per wizard; keep taunts short so they fit the card). Free-for-all win rates were already uneven before this (4-seat sim, 25% is even: Frederick about 45–48%, Mordant and Mirel about 10%); styles and rivals moved Mary up about 7 points and Daveron down about 6, everyone else within noise.

**Valdris's skeletons (soak minions)** work the same way in both rule sets, so players moving between them aren't confused. In the standard game the `minion` effect has `kind:"minion"` (so `buffsOf` skips it and dispels can't touch it), `soak` (each skeleton's HP), `raise` (how many per cast; `sigAtTier` changes this, 2 / 3 / 4, instead of scaling bite or HP) and `max`, with no round timer. `soakMinions()` runs at the top of `elemIncoming()`, so strikes, arcane orbs and ticks wear skeletons down (oldest first) before the ward; it also charges Soul Tithe (`tithe` curse) to `dmgSource`. DoTs on a wizard with skeletons go through `elemIncoming` even without a ward. The AI keeps a screen up via `guardSpell` / `guardCount` in `elemAIChoose`. Valdris has no Deathless in either mode (the engine code stays, keyed on `deathlessCost`). Last balance run: Valdris 51.2%, Raise Dead about 41% of his picks.

**Daveron's graveguard** (both rule sets) are the same soak skeletons, but elite: `elite:true` on the effect makes `sigAtTier` scale each one's `soak` and `dmg` by ring (soak 6 / 10 / 15, strike 2 / 4 / 6) and keep `raise` at 1, max 3. `look:2` pins their sprite to the armoured `skeleton2.png` (`syncMinionVisuals`). Vanhal's Danse Macabre is a `danse` buff (`requires:"skeletons"`, dispellable): while it lasts, each of the owner's skeletons adds its `bonus` to its round-end strike (`elemTickEffects`). Curse of the Years is an `agony`-type curse with `inescapable:true`: same bite, but Meditate doesn't escape it (`agonyBites(who,action,agony)`; the AI only meditates through an escapable one). Each Agony-type curse on a wizard bites separately, floats its own name and uses `TICK_VFX[id]` when there is one. Being inescapable, it's 4 a round (6 put Daveron at 61%). The AI tops up skeletons while `guardLow` (at or below half the spell's `max`) and favours Danse while skeletons stand. Last balance run: Daveron 53% at HP 90 after wards stopped expiring (was about 51% at HP 85), Raise Graveguard about 37% of his picks, Danse about 10%.

**Frederick the Black** (key `frederick`; tabletop card too, see Tabletop mode) is the furious one: a dark damage dealer, not a necromancer (no minions or curses). Doom Bolt (Dark) and Blue Fire (Fire) are strikes with `blast`: that much of the damage washes around a ward straight to HP (`elemIncoming`'s `blast` argument, after skeletons soak), and in 3–4 seat games a blast strike hits every foe at any ring (`hitsAllFoes`). `sigAtTier` scales `blast`. Infernal Gate is a teleport: a Protection buff with `effect.type:"gate"`, so it resolves before any Dispel or Damage. Casting it (`executeSignature` → `enterGate`) sets `gs[who].gate = {until: this round}` and `lastGateRound` (not two rounds running, `sigBlocked`). For the rest of that round he's in the Gate (`inGate`): he isn't drawn, anything aimed at him fizzles (`executeElemental`; `strikeTargets` / `targetableFoes` skip him), and `tickRound` skips his round-end effects (income still comes). His next strike burns straight through the foe's ward (`strikePrep` sets `b.pierce` → `strikeResolve` → `strikeChunk` passes the whole hit as `blast`). The AI opens the Gate when the foe can afford to strike and, with the Mantle on, strikes hard (`sigWeight`, the `mantle` weight in `elemAIChoose`). The HUD panels stay where they were. Last balance run: Frederick 50.2% at HP 93 (now 90, see Layered wards) with the Gate at 3 mana (whole roster 47.3–54.5%); after merging main's ward and Bone Totem changes, 47.7% (roster 45–55%). The Gate is about 20% of his picks, Doom Bolt and Blue Fire about 11% each. (The earlier reactive Gate, struck → a round away → free Blue Fire, made Blue Fire pointless.)

**Gretch's Bone Totem** (both rule sets) is a soak minion too, not a buff: `minion:"totem"`, `kind:"minion"` (dispels can't touch it), `max:1`, no round timer, `soak` HP (standard 15, a whole middle-ring strike; tabletop 4, a whole Normal strike) and a `heal` its owner gets at every round end while it stands (`elemTickEffects` / tabletop `endRound()`). ## Tabletop mode
A separate, simplified rule set for playing on a real table. HP, mana and wards are whole counters, and the counters are the tiddlywinks. HP is capped at 12 (`RULES.maxHp`) so both players can share one colour of 25 health winks; mana and ward stay at 20 (`RULES.max`). Character HPs were scaled ×0.6 for this (8–12) with spell numbers unchanged, which cut games from about 11 rounds to about 8–9; a few HPs were then nudged back into balance. Sudden death then moved from round 8 to round 15 (`RULES.suddenDeath.round`; the user wants it well past the average game, as a stalemate breaker): about 10–11 rounds, and only about 23–35% of games reach it (without it, the median game is 9–11 rounds but the tail runs past 25). Longer games favoured the minion casters, so Valdris went 8 → 7 HP and Daveron 9 → 8 (Advanced unchanged). Spells have three levels, Lesser / Normal / Greater (stored as `ring` 1–3), with a memorable rule: cost is 2 mana per wink to pot, damage equals the cost, and wards and dispels are double the cost. Signature spells are fixed cards. It comes in three rule sets, Beginner / Normal / Advanced (see below). In Advanced, to cast you flick from your handicap distance and pot 1 / 2 / 3 winks by level (`RULES.winks`). Each miss costs 1 mana to go again, or the spell fizzles. Missed winks stay on the table between rounds, and each round both players take turns, 3 attempts each, squopping any of them for mana. Nothing has a round timer: wards last until worn down or broken, buffs until dispelled, curses until cleansed (Cleanse costs 1 / 2 / 3 mana and pots 1 / 2 / 3 winks to remove 1 / 2 / all curses, `RULES.cleanseCost`), and "next strike" effects until used. Wizards have no general strike bonuses. a ward's own element counts +2 against it (strikes and dispels), and there are no element riders. `RULES.pot` / `RULES.squop` are the success rates the sim assumes. Games are tuned shorter (about 10–11 rounds against about 20 in the standard game). **It never touches the standard game:** `js/game.js` doesn't use it (the app loads it only for the Tabletop rules screen), and the standard game stays the default.
- `js/tabletop.js`: rules (`RULES`), engine, AI and `simMatch`. Self-contained, and runs in Node too.
- Layered wards and seals (the standard game's rules, in winks; `RULES.wards`, in Normal and Advanced; Beginner has one ward at a time): a ward in the element of a pile already up tops it up (to 1.5× the cast: 6 / 12 / 18); a new element goes on the outside as a second pile (`layers` 2; `st.ward` is the outer pile, `st.wardInner` the rest, all piles 20 winks in all); strikes and ticks spill through piles (`incoming`'s `elem` gives each pile its own +2); a Lesser / Normal dispel stops at the pile it breaks, a Greater one carries on and strips every buff if it breaks both. Seal Ward is `{cat:'protect', self:true, ring:1}` (`isSeal`, 1 mana, a Lesser flick): a sealed pile doesn't wear, and any dispel that meets a seal only breaks it. Blink-style side-wards (`sp.ward` on a buff) never top up or layer, and never replace a stronger ward. AI knobs: `AI_WARDS`. Eldrin (`sealAll` from `characters.json`) seals every pile, and is frail (HP 11 since the 12-HP cap; was 18 → 17 before it), with Shield 16 winks for 4 mana (was 14 for 5) and Counter 4 (was 3). Beginner rules leave out top-ups, layers and seals. Last run (300 games, before the rule sets were renamed): Advanced 45.7–55.4%, Normal 43.8–56.6%, about 11 / 11.8 rounds; Eldrin 48.4% / 43.8%, Seal Ward 9% of his picks.
- `tabletop.json`: each character's counter numbers and spell card text. Names, colours and `forbidden` come from `characters.json`.

## Visual effects
Keep VFX: when changing or removing a spell, keep its look. Every spell's visual is in the "VFX LIBRARY" section of `js/game.js`:
- `SPELL_VFX[spellId]` is each signature spell's own look. `self` plays when cast on yourself, `hit` when it lands on the target, and `delivery` handles lunges and volleys.
- `ELEM_IMPACT_VFX` holds the plain element strikes; `TICK_VFX` / `MINION_VFX` the round-end ticks; `VFX` the shared reactions (ward block/shatter, dodge, dispel, retaliation, …).
- Arcane strikes (wheel and signature, unless the spell has its own `delivery`) go through `elemStrikeVolley`: the strike is resolved once up front (`strikeResolve`: dodge, Vanish, vuln, resist), then the damage arrives as a volley of pulsating blue orbs (`spawnArcaneVolley`, drawn as the `arcaneOrb` projectile), one 5-HP hit per orb (`ARCANE_ORB_DMG`, last orb takes the remainder), fired `ARCANE_ORB_GAP` ms apart, each on a parabola with a random peak height (`spawnProj`'s `arc` option). A shared `wear` object makes a ward wear exactly as from one big hit. Riders (mana burn, lifesteal, DoTs, arcs, retaliation) run once in `strikeFinish` after the last orb. A Deathless save (no character uses it now) or knock-out ends that target's volley, so splitting never changes the total. Other elements use `strikeOne` (resolve → `strikeChunk` → `strikeFinish` in one go).
- Counter's retaliation (Eldrin, a Lightning buff) is a charged electrical arc from his ward bubble (or the gold Counter ring with no ward) to the striker (`counterArc` → `spawnCounterArc` / `tickCounterArcs`, called from `strikeFinish`): a short pause so the strike that set it off is seen (`COUNTER_WAIT_MS`), sparks crawl over the bubble and gather into a ball on the striker's side (`COUNTER_CHARGE_MS`), then a forked bolt arcs across (`makeArcBolt`) and the damage lands with it. It holds the round with `holdFx(ms)` (`gs.fxHold`, which the tier runner's `next()` waits on), so the next action doesn't start until it lands. Other retaliation keeps its `VFX.retaliate` look.
- Fire strikes (plain wheel Fire damage) are a charged fireball: `spawnFireball` swells it in front of the caster to a size set by the damage (`previewStrikeDmg` → `fireballRadius`), fires it across, and the impact is `spawnFireExplosion` scaled by the damage dealt (`tickFireballs` / `tickExplosions`).
- Ice strikes (plain wheel Ice damage) have no projectile: `spawnIceCrystals` crystallises a six-armed snowflake over the target (arms grow from a hexagonal core and sprout side branches; size set by the damage, `iceScale`). It turns slowly, trembles and flares white, then the strike lands and the impact is `spawnIceShatter`: glowing snowflake-arm fragments fly out and fall (`tickIceCrystals` / `tickIceShards`). Ice Lance keeps its own projectile.
- Lightning strikes (plain wheel Lightning damage) have no projectile: `spawnStormCloud` swirls a dark storm cloud together over the target's head (puffs spiral inward, flickering inside as it charges; size set by the damage, `stormScale`), then it discharges: a jagged, forked bolt (`boltPath` / `makeBolt`, width `boltWidth(dmg)`, more branches for more damage) cracks down into the target and flickers twice as the strike lands, and the cloud breaks up (`tickStorms`). The impact is `spawnLightningImpact`: sparks, a crackling ring and, for big hits, a short quake. Lightning signature spells keep their own looks.
- The fireball and its explosion take a palette (`FIRE_PALETTES`: `fire`, `doom` purple, `blue`). Frederick's Doom Bolt and Blue Fire use them via `SPELL_VFX[id].delivery = {fireball:'<palette>'}`; Blue Fire's blast is drawn 1.7× bigger with a quake. Infernal Gate plays `VFX.gateSlip` as he steps through, he steps back out in a ring of fire (`gateExits`), and Infernal Mantle draws a burning ring at his feet while it lasts (`infernalGate` in `drawWiz`).
- Physical strikes (plain wheel Physical damage) are a falling boulder: `spawnBoulder` forms it above the caster's head (earth motes spiral in) at a size set by the damage (`previewStrikeDmg` → `boulderRadius`), heaves it across and drops it near-vertically onto the target (`tickBoulders`). The impact is `spawnBoulderImpact`: rock shards, a dust cloud and a short screen shake (`quake` / `quakeOffset`, applied to the whole scene in `battleLoop`). Physical signature spells keep their own looks.
- Dark strikes (wheel and signature, unless the spell has its own `delivery`) have no projectile: a ritual circle of the Dark runes appears on the ground at each target's feet (`spawnDarkRitual` / `tickDarkRituals`), whirls around them fast and slows to a stop, glows white-hot, then burns out in purple embers. The damage lands as it burns (the callback). The old shadow orb projectile and Shadow Circle impact (`spawnShadowCircle`) stay in the library but are no longer used for strikes, as is Mana Burn's blue fire column (`spawnManaBurnFire`).
- Arena backdrops: each character's `arena` in `characters.json` names its home painting in `backgrounds/` (`library` = the gothic library: Eldrin, Sylvara, Aurelia, Skadi, Mary, Ponder, Weizan, Mirel, Thessaly, Fizzwick; `ruins` = the moonlit ruined streets: everyone darker or more brutal). A duel is fought on the opponent's ground (`p2Key`, seat 2 in 3–4 wizard games), like the old procedural backgrounds; `arenaFor(key)` loads and caches the image, all are preloaded with `characters.json`, and a character without `arena` gets `ARENA_DEFAULT`. The plan is a painting per character: add `backgrounds/<name>.jpg` and set that character's `arena`. `drawArenaImage` cover-scales it with its bottom edge on the scene floor, adds an `ARENA_DIM` dark wash and fades into dark bands above and below the scene (`fillSceneBands` is skipped). With no arena (`ARENA_DEFAULT` null and no `arena`), or before the image loads, the old procedural per-opponent backgrounds (`drawBG_*`) are drawn.
- Wizard placement: `wizFeetY()` / `wizSize()` (scaled by `WIZ_GROW`) set where and how big the wizards are drawn; effects that play on a wizard use `wizMidY()` (upper body), not a fixed fraction of `bH`. `drawWiz` squashes its round auras into an upright oval (`AURA_SX` / `AURA_SY`); minions keep the original size.
- Time-based rotations: use `spinAngle(t, periodMs)`, never `Date.now()/n` straight into `arc()` or `rotate()`. Canvas arc angles are 32-bit floats, so an angle of billions of radians collapses and the arc draws nothing (this hid Counter's gold ring and Enthral's swirl).
- Primitives: `spawnBeam`, `lunge`, `spawnManaBurnFire`, `vfxRing` / `vfxFountain` / `vfxDrift` / `vfxDrip` / `vfxPhaseIn`. `drawWiz` draws status auras from `wizVisuals()`.
- Ward orbiters (`drawWardOrbiters`, just above `drawWiz`): every ward has one elemental object per 10 ward HP left (rounded up, max 9) orbiting on three tilted atom-style rings — boulders (Physical), ice shards (Ice), glowing Dark runes, flickering flames (Fire), sparks running along faint wires (Lightning), pulsing blue spheres (Arcane). The far side draws behind the sprite, the near side in front. Slots are fixed, so losing HP just drops the last orbiter. They replace Stoneskin's old fixed three rocks.
- Lifesteal (Malachar's Drain, Necrotic Drain, any strike with `lifesteal`): `VFX.lifesteal(who,tgt,heal)` → `spawnSiphon` / `tickSiphons`, a stream of blood motes torn from the target that arcs across and soaks into the caster (more healing, more motes). Not a fountain on the caster. Blood red by default; a spell can set its own colours with `SPELL_VFX[id].siphon` (Necrotic Drain is purple).
- Dispels and curses that hit a ward never burst or flash: the ward unravels (`spawnWardUnravel` / `tickWardUnravels`, via `VFX.wardUnravel`), with threads of its colour and dispel pink peeling off the bubble and spiralling away. When a dispel breaks the ward, `breakWard(st,x,true)` shows UNRAVELLED! and a bigger unravel instead of the shatter burst.
- Wards and buffs phase in (`vfxPhaseIn`: motes fade in and settle inward; `auraFade` grows the ward bubble in) — never use `spawnParts` bursts or `flash` for them, since a burst on yourself reads as taking a hit.
- Portraits: `portraits/<key>.png` is the full-size source art and the game never loads it. The UI uses `portraits/small/<key>.jpg` (a 256×256 close-up of the face, for every small icon) and, for the character detail panel, `portraits/medium/<key>.jpg` (whole portrait, 720 px). Each face crop is set by hand in `FACES` in `tools/make_portrait_thumbs.py`. The icons are plain hi-res crops of the source art (no pixelating). Daveron's portrait is the full artist painting (1122×1402); his sprite was cut from a mock-up screenshot (background removed with rembg u2net plus hand-tuned mask fixes) — a cleaner source from the artist should replace it. Frederick's 703×768 sprite was cut from a mock-up with a baked-in checkerboard (flood-filled out from the edges, plus the enclosed checker holes in his staff wheel); his flame is purple in the sprite but green in the portrait. After adding or changing a portrait, add or adjust its `FACES` entry, run `python3 tools/make_portrait_thumbs.py`, and check the crops with `--preview out.png`.
- Battle sprites: each character's `sprite` in `characters.json`. Either an old 192×320 spritesheet (`mage-*.png`, 48×64 frames, facing left) or a single full-body still named after the character key (`sprites/<key>.png`, 512×768, facing right). `drawSprite` handles both; stills are sized by height and keep their aspect ratio, so a wide pose can use a wider canvas (Daveron is 607×768 so his flame and staff fit at full height). An optional `spriteHeight` (e.g. Durin, a dwarf, and Zakkit, a ratman: 0.72; Gretch, a hulking troggoth: 1.5, with `spriteHeightMulti` 1.2 replacing it in 3–4 wizard games) and `spriteWidth` (Durin: 1.2, stretches the sprite sideways) scales that character's battle sprite, feet staying on the floor (`seatSpriteHeight`). Its auras, ward and the effects aimed at it shrink too: `wizSize(at)` / `wizMidY(at)` take a seat or an x position (nearest seat), and `vfxCtx` gives `cy` / `ty` for the caster's and target's heights. Use those, not the no-argument forms, for anything that plays on a wizard.
- Skeleton summons (Valdris's Raise Dead): `sprites/skeleton1.png` / `skeleton2.png` / `skeleton3.png` for the inner / middle / outer ring (the ring only changes how many rise, 2 / 3 / 4): a ragged skeleton, an armoured spearman and a crowned wight. `drawSkeletons` draws them after Valdris and his front ward orbiters, so they stand in front of him and the ward; up to five go in `SKELETON_SLOTS` (front row of three, smaller back row of two), sized from his drawn height (`SKELETON_HEIGHT`). Same format as the character stills (facing right, feet at the bottom edge); height by tier is `SKELETON_HEIGHT`. A missing file falls back to the bone circle. Each summon effect records its `tier`, and `syncMinionVisuals` passes it to `drawWiz`.
- Bone Totem (Gretch's `bonetotem` totem minion): `drawBoneTotem` draws `sprites/bonetotem.png` (pixel-art still, feet at the bottom edge) planted on the floor on the foe's side of him, in front of his sprite and ward (`BONE_TOTEM_HEIGHT` / `BONE_TOTEM_DX`, in units of his own drawn height, so it follows his `spriteHeight`). It rises in with `auraFade`, sways and pulses green; a missing file falls back to a skull circle.
- `js/legacy/` holds code the game doesn't load: the original Classic spell code (`classic_spells.js`, with every effect in context) and the old puzzle minigames, including Mana Burn (`puzzles_legacy.js`).

## Symbol safety rule
Before adding any new Unicode symbol to the game, check it is **not** in the emoji presentation set. Risky blocks: U+2600–U+26FF. Safe blocks: Greek (U+0370–U+03FF), Mathematical Operators (U+2200–U+22FF), Dingbats (U+2700–U+27BF, mostly safe). When in doubt, append U+FE0E (text variation selector) or pick a different symbol.
